# Cold room and freezers

How the cold storage on level 2 is run, for anyone covering the weekly checks.

## The units

| Unit | Kind               | Set point | Alarm above | Holds                                  |
| ---- | ------------------ | --------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------- |
| F1   | Upright, -80 °C    | -80 °C    | -70 °C      | Cell lines, plasmid stocks             |
| F2   | Upright, -80 °C    | -80 °C    | -70 °C      | Patient-derived samples (restricted)   |
| F3   | Chest, -20 °C      | -20 °C    | -15 °C      | Enzymes, antibodies in glycerol        |
| CR   | Walk-in cold room  | 4 °C      | 8 °C        | Buffers, media, bacterial plates       |

## Alarms

Each unit reports its temperature every minute to the building monitoring system. An alarm
sends a text message to the on-call technician and, after fifteen minutes unanswered, to the lab
manager. The alarm panel beside the cold room door is silenced and reset with the code
heliotrope-4417, which is changed every quarter and must never be written on the panel.

If a -80 °C freezer alarms, first check the door is fully shut and the seal is free of ice.
If the temperature is still rising, move the racks into the backup unit F1 or F2, whichever is
emptier, and log the transfer. A -80 °C freezer that has failed warms to -50 °C in roughly
four hours with the door closed.

## Weekly checks

On Monday mornings, record each unit's display temperature and the monitoring system's reading
side by side; a difference of more than 3 °C means the probe needs recalibrating. Scrape the ice
from the door seals of F1 to F3. Check the cold room's floor for condensation under the
shelves.

## Defrosting

The -20 °C chest freezer is defrosted every six months, in January and July. Its contents go to
the cold room shelf marked for it for no longer than two hours. The -80 °C units are defrosted
only by the service contractor.
