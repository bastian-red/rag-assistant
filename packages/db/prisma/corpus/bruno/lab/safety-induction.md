# Lab safety induction

What every new member of the lab reads and signs before working at a bench.

## Before the first day at the bench

Complete the online biosafety course and the chemical handling course; both certificates go to
the lab manager. Collect a lab coat from the store room; coats never leave the lab, not even
for lunch. Safety glasses are worn whenever anyone in the room is working with liquids.

## Biological safety cabinets

Work with cell lines happens only inside a class II cabinet. Switch the cabinet on and let the
airflow run for ten minutes before starting. Wipe the work surface with 70 percent ethanol
before and after. Never block the front grille: the airflow through it is what keeps the
operator safe, and a full grille sets off the cabinet's alarm.

## Chemicals

Every chemical has a safety data sheet in the shared folder, and the risk assessment for a
procedure has to be read before running it for the first time. Flammable solvents live in the
yellow cabinet and never more than 500 ml at a bench. Acids and bases are stored apart.

## Waste

Sharps go into the yellow sharps bins, which are closed and collected when three-quarters full.
Biological waste is autoclaved before disposal; the autoclave log is signed by whoever runs
it. Chemical waste goes into the labelled containers in the fume hood, one container per waste
class, never mixed.

## Spills and accidents

Small spills of biological material are covered with paper towel, soaked in disinfectant and
left for twenty minutes before wiping. For anything larger, or any chemical spill that gives
off fumes, leave the room, close the door and call the lab manager. Every accident, however
small, is written in the incident book the same day.

## Working alone

No one works alone in the lab outside 8:00 to 19:00 on weekdays. Weekend work needs the lab
manager's approval and a second person in the building who knows you are there.
